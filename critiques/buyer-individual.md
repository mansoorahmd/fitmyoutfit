# Critique: First-time individual gift buyer (1–2 tees or a jersey, no design skills, comparing Custom Ink / Zazzle / Printful)

Reviewed: C:\Users\baapo\source\repos\FitMyOutfit\plan.md (read in full). Assumed buyer location: US, the market the plan worries about most. Where UK changes the picture, I say so.

## Top 5 failures (ranked)

### 1. The landed price for one shirt is uncompetitive, and the plan never says what I will actually pay
**What breaks:** I price one tee at Custom Ink and Zazzle, then at you. Their total includes delivery. Yours has to absorb US duty, a carrier processing fee and express air freight from Lahore. If the "instant all-in price" isn't there on day one, a courier bill will hit me after delivery, and that is the 1-star review. The plan's only mitigation (Risk #3) is to "test DDP pricing on 10 real quotes before the M6 launch, and lead with bulk group shipments". That does nothing for a single-piece buyer. The plan also doesn't record which tariff rate applies, even though the rate has changed three times in 12 months.

**Evidence:**
- Custom Ink lists one Gildan Ultra Cotton tee at $29.03 including one ink color, free delivery and free artwork review ([Custom Ink no-minimum tees](https://www.customink.com/products/t-shirts/no-minimum-t-shirts/198)).
- Zazzle-printed basic tees start around $20–26 ([Picsart/Zazzle tee listing](https://picsart.com/print/shop/t-shirts/)). Printful's US shipping for a first tee is about $4–5 (third-party figures that disagree with each other; [Printful shipping](https://printful.com/shipping), [Avada](https://avada.io/blog/printful-pricing/)).
- The US base (MFN) duty on cotton tees under HTS 6109.10 is 16.5% ([Wove tariff lookup](https://tariffs.wove.com/us/tariff/6109.10)).
- Since 24 July 2026, Pakistan-origin goods pay an extra 10% under Section 301 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-united-states-imposes-section-301-tariffs-on-60-economies-failing-to-address-forced-labor.html), [Daily Pakistan](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/24-Jul-2026/pakistan-faces-10pc-us-tariff-under-new-forced-labor-trade-policy)). That 10% replaced a 10% Section 122 surcharge that expired that day ([UHY](https://uhy-us.com/insights/news/2026/july/tariff-reset-section-122-expires-as-section-301-duties-expand)). The Section 122 surcharge had itself replaced the 19% IEEPA rate, which the Supreme Court struck down on 20 Feb 2026 ([Wipfli](https://www.wipfli.com/insights/articles/supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-released), [Dawn on the 19% rate](https://www.dawn.com/news/1927992)).
- A Pakistani apparel brand still tells US buyers the duty is "approximately 29%", that duties "are the responsibility of the recipient", and that free shipping covers "courier charges only" ([Studio by TCS](https://www.studiobytcs.com/pages/u-s-customers-import-duties-customs-information)). Sellers in your own city are quoting rates that no longer apply.
- FedEx and UPS added a $2.50 inbound processing fee. One carrier executive puts handling and brokerage at $15–34 per parcel ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ups-fedex-increase-fees-us-imports-end-of-de-minimis/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3037973)).
- A Texas Jackets buyer got a FedEx invoice for "custom duties and taxes" about five months after buying ([Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/texasjackets.com)).
- Evidence gap: I could not find a reliable published 2026 express rate for 0.5 kg from Lahore to the US. The only figure I found ($40–90 for under 2 kg) came from a low-quality site, so I'm not relying on it.

**Opinion:** On a $25 declared value, 26.5% duty plus a processing fee adds about $9 before any freight. Express air from Lahore is likely to cost more than Custom Ink's entire price. If I have to type my address before I see a total, I leave.

**What the plan should change:**
- Make the single-piece DDP price (product + duty + fees + freight) a launch blocker with a dollar target, such as "within 20% of Custom Ink's 1-piece price".
- Show it on the product page before I design anything.
- Guarantee in writing that I will never get a courier bill, and refund it if I do.
- Store the tariff stack (MFN + Section 301 + any surcharge) as data you can update in a day.
- Decide now whether to batch single US orders into one consolidated shipment and send the last mile from inside the US. Shipping each tee alone by express from Lahore probably can't hit the price.
- Move the "10 real quotes" test from before M6 to M2.

### 2. Gift deadline: no arrive-by date, no rush option, and a mandatory human review step in the way
**What breaks:** A gift has a date. Competitors give me one at checkout and refund me if they miss it. Your plan promises "a turnaround counted from proof approval". As a gift buyer I don't know or care when proof approval happens. Every order also gets a human artwork review (Must for launch, M2), then air freight, then US customs. The plan even argues that "buyers forgive slow delivery far more readily than wrong colors or wrong sizes". That's backed by team-jersey reviews, not gift buyers.

**Evidence:**
- Zazzle: standard production 1–3 business days plus 3–7 days US shipping ([Picsart/Zazzle](https://picsart.com/print/shop/t-shirts/)). Its Zip service caps production at 1 day with delivery in 1–2 business days ([Zazzle shipping info via search](https://www.zazzle.com/tshirts)).
- Custom Ink: two-week standard delivery, one-week rush for +15%, three business days for +30% ([Custom Ink delivery options](https://www.customink.com/help_center/delivery-options), [FAQ](https://www.customink.com/help_center/faq-when-will-i-receive-my-order)).
- Custom Ink's guarantee offers a full refund within 15 days if goods "arrive late" ([Custom Ink guarantee](https://www.customink.com/guarantee); the page returned 403 to my fetch, so the terms come from the search result summary).
- Printful: 3–4 business days US transit after fulfillment ([Printful support](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396648644497-What-are-Printful-s-shipping-options)).
- Texas Jackets reviewer: "paid for express shipping", item arrived late ([Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/texasjackets.com)).

**Opinion:** If checkout can't show "arrives by [date]", I won't buy a birthday gift from you. A human review step with no stated SLA is an unbounded delay for a one-shirt order.

**What the plan should change:**
- Put a guaranteed arrive-by date, worked out from my address, at checkout before payment, with an automatic refund if you miss it.
- Give single DTF/DTG orders a human-review SLA (for example 4 business hours) and skip review for template-only designs with no uploads.
- Offer a paid rush tier.
- Publish a cut-off date for each holiday.

### 3. Wrong size on a gift: no exchange, no claim flow at launch, and a "fit kit" that makes no sense for one shirt
**What breaks:**
- I usually don't know the recipient's exact size. The plan's answer is garment-measurement charts, a fit kit, and "remakes only for our errors". For one shirt that ends up a gift that doesn't fit and can't be fixed.
- The plan has no fallback for a bad guess. Shipping a return to Lahore would cost more than the shirt.
- The "Remake and claim flow with photo upload and reason codes" is listed as Soon after launch, not Must for launch, in the gaps table (line 102). Yet the Purchaser POV section names that flow as the answer to "you're wrong" disputes. At launch, a color or misprint complaint has no proper path.

**Evidence:**
- Zazzle says size and fit issues are covered by the Zazzle Promise for 30 days after receipt ([Zazzle help: returns](https://www.zazzle.com.au/hc/article/how_to_return_an_item-220294868)).
- Custom Ink's guarantee covers material differences, flaws and late delivery, but not my own sizing mistake ([Custom Ink guarantee](https://www.customink.com/guarantee)). A Custom Ink reviewer said a wrong-size shirt "can't be returned" ([Custom Ink reviews](https://www.customink.com/reviews?page=431)).
- Printful gives no reprint or refund for a wrong size or color the customer chose ([Printful refunds](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396595299729)).
- Even Zazzle draws complaints about replacements arriving in the wrong size and refunds being denied ([Trustpilot: Zazzle](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.zazzle.com?page=10)).

**Opinion:**
- Zazzle sets the bar for gift buyers, and you're up against it from a factory 11,000 km away.
- A fit kit makes sense for a 30-player team. For a $30 gift it's absurd.
- If I learn after the fact that "made-to-order, no exchanges" covers a size I guessed wrong, I'll write a 1-star review saying I was misled, even though I ticked the box.

**What the plan should change:**
- For single pieces, include one free size remake with no return required (the buyer keeps or donates the wrong one) and price it into the single-piece margin.
- Add a "pick a shirt you already own and enter its chest width" sizing helper.
- Move the photo claim flow to Must for launch (M2/M4, not after launch).
- State the size policy on the product page, not only in a checkbox at checkout.

### 4. Trust: unknown brand, ships from Pakistan, no PayPal, and origin I may only discover on delivery
**What breaks:**
- The only trust work in the plan is "reviews" in M6.
- There is no plan for disclosing where the goods are made, no buyer-protected way to pay, no stated support response time, and no plan for the cold-start review problem.
- The plan itself says PayPal and Stripe aren't directly available from Pakistan. That removes the payment method I would use with an unknown store.
- Merchants who hide where they ship from get punished in reviews.

**Evidence:**
- Texas Jackets has a 3.6 TrustScore from 96 reviews, 37% of them 1-star. The 1-star reviews include "Based out of Pakistan not Texas" and "it came from Pakistan!" The seller's replies ("If we were a scam company, you would not have received it at all") made things worse ([Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/texasjackets.com)).
- A buyer at a Pakistani custom-shirt store paid in advance, waited over three weeks, and wrote "Now they are NOT RESPONDING" ([Trustpilot: The Shoppies](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/theshoppies.pk)).
- Gateway limits: plan.md, Seller POV table, "Gateway and payout friction from Pakistan".

**Opinion:**
- An unknown .com that charges my card through a foreign acquirer, maybe with a foreign-transaction fee, and offers no PayPal is a reason to leave before I even design.
- If I find out the shirt comes from Lahore only when the tracking page says so, I'll feel deceived even if the product is good.

**What the plan should change:**
- Market the factory openly: "made in our own Lahore factory" with photos, video and named staff.
- Show a delivered-price example on the homepage.
- Decide the "destination-market entity" question before launch. A US merchant of record or PayPal-capable entity is a conversion requirement, not a back-office choice.
- Publish a support SLA, such as a reply within 12 hours.
- Collect photo reviews from the M5 pilot so the store doesn't open with zero reviews.
- Make the factory-fed order-status page (now M4) visible from the moment I pay.

### 5. Time to a good-looking design: thin template library, AI deferred, and a 3D editor as the front door
**What breaks:**
- With no design skills, I need to land on a finished design and change the name, date or photo.
- The plan has one graphic designer building the whole template, clipart and font library, across three products and every occasion, during M1 (weeks 3–10), while also staffing the design-for-me queue.
- AI design assist is "Soon after launch" and only reaches beta in the M5 pilot. Yet the plan's own landscape table says "AI design help is becoming expected for individual buyers".
- The M1 exit criterion is that an individual "finishes" a design without help. That says nothing about whether the result looks good or how long it takes.
- Fixing low-resolution uploads by hand is a paid service, so my blurry family photo either prints badly or costs me extra.

**Evidence:**
- Zazzle lets shoppers "personalize millions of designs from Independent Creators" through simple fill-in fields on the product page ([Zazzle maker tees](https://www.zazzle.com/maker+tshirts), [Zazzle template help](https://help.zazzle.com/hc/en-gb/articles/28113286236951-Personalising-Customising-Your-Product-Using-Templates-on-the-Product-Page)).
- Custom Ink has thousands of templates and clipart organized by occasion, and its artist review is free ([Custom Ink guide](https://www.customink.com/blog/?p=26229), [Custom Ink no-minimum listing](https://www.customink.com/products/t-shirts/no-minimum-t-shirts/198)).
- Zazzle reviewers still complain about blurry, pixelated enlarged photos and colors lighter than the preview ([Trustpilot: Zazzle](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.zazzle.com?page=9)), so upload quality is where gift buyers get burned.

**Opinion:**
- A 3D editor impresses a coach. To me it's friction, especially on a phone.
- A few hundred templates from one designer will look thin next to Zazzle's millions.
- If my first five minutes are a blank 3D garment, I'm gone.

**What the plan should change:**
- Make fill-in-fields gift templates the default entry for individuals, with the full editor one click away.
- Set a launch target for templates per occasion (for example 50+ each for birthday, family, memorial and sports-fan) and add a second designer or licensed template packs.
- Add a success metric: median time from landing to cart under 5 minutes in user tests.
- Make the automatic photo upscale and fix free for single orders.
- Pull a basic AI "describe it, get three starting designs" feature into M1/M2 if individuals are the first segment.

## What the plan gets right for me (max 3 bullets)
- **Instant all-in (DDP) price at checkout is treated as Must for launch (M2).** That is exactly what would stop the surprise courier bill that sinks other Pakistan-based sellers. It just has to be real for one piece, not only for bulk orders.
- **Print-tested palette with HEX snapping and a warning, a stored signed-off proof, and a human artwork review.** These go straight at the "colors lighter than the preview" and "seller says I'm wrong" complaints that even Zazzle gets.
- **An order-status page fed by factory stages instead of manual emails.** For a buyer waiting on a parcel from abroad, live, honest status is the single biggest reassurance.

## One question I'd need answered before buying
If I order one custom T-shirt today to a US address, what is the exact total I pay at checkout, with nothing billed at my door, by what guaranteed date will it arrive, and what happens if it doesn't fit?
