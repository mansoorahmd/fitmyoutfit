This is a modified version of the work. What we changed: scripts/process-stand-in-model.ts keeps
only the outer cloth surface of the garment and its trims (it drops the inner copy of the cloth
and the edge strips that joined the two), removes both textures (the tiger-print base colour and
the normal map) and both materials and puts one plain white material in their place, splits the
cloth into 27 pattern pieces and renames them, measures the UV layout against the 3D surface and
re-lays it in millimetres at one scale, packs the pieces into a new UV layout, turns the model from
Z up in centimetres to Y up in metres and stands it on Y = 0, welds the seam vertices, recomputes
the normals, adds baked ambient occlusion (_OCCLUSION), joins all pieces into one mesh and stores
the vertex data as integers (KHR_mesh_quantization). The garment's shape is unchanged.

The source file's SHA-256 is recorded in stand-in-jersey.source.json. It is a stand-in until our
own pattern-based models arrive.
